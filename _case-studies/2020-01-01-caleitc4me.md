### Prioritizing Interactive Tools for Action

As the designer on this project, my top priorities were the two interactive tools at the heart of the user experience: a tax credit calculator and a searchable database of free tax preparation events.

Rather than treating these as secondary resources buried within the site, I redesigned the experience to bring both tools directly onto the homepage and into reusable compontents that could be surfaced throughout the site as needed. This made the most important actions immediately visible to visitors and reduced the need to navigate through multiple pages to find assistance.

  <div class="cs-image-block image-radius-standard image-inner-shadow image-inner-shadow-light">
    <img src="/assets/design/2020-caleitc4me-02-map-02.jpg" alt="An example of the Tax Prep Assistance locator map on the 2019 tax season refresh of CalEITC4Me.org, designed by Stephen Cummings" loading="lazy" />
  </div>

For the calculator, I focused on reducing friction and making potential benefits immediately understandable. While early concepts explored breaking the experience into a series of smaller steps, implementation realities led us toward a simpler solution: integrating and restyling the third-party calculator to feel native to the site. To streamline the process, I worked with developers to pass information entered on the homepage directly into the calculator, reducing duplicate data entry and helping users move more seamlessly through the experience. I also improved mobile responsiveness and redesigned the results display to emphasize estimated benefits through clearer typography, hierarchy, and visual presentation.

  <div class="cs-image-block image-radius-standard">
    <img src="/assets/design/2020-caleitc4me-03-calculator-02.jpg" alt="An example of tax benefit estimates from CalEITC4Me's benefits calculator, with typographic hierarchy designed by Stephen Cummings" loading="lazy" />
  </div>

For the tax assistance locator, I designed an interface that paired a searchable map with clear event information, indicators for recurring versus limited-time services, and calendar integration options to help visitors plan appointments and access free filing support throughout California.

</section>

### Supporting Ten Languages at Scale

Every feature on the site, from navigation and content modules to the tax calculator and event locator, needed to function across multiple languages, including right-to-left languages such as Farsi.

Working with my colleagues to structure the site, I helped create custom CMS tools that allowed editors to manage language by language content without getting lost in a sea of duplicative pages. Using modular design components, language-specific post types, content delivery conditioned on URL structure, and shared translation variables, we built an experience that remained consistent while accommodating the needs of editors and users in each language version.

This approach reduced editorial overhead while ensuring that critical tax information remained accessible to diverse communities across California.

### Building a Flexible Editorial System

One of the organization's biggest challenges was content publishing. Maintaining visual consistency on the outgoing site required direct edits to HTML and CSS, creating a workflow where only a single staff member could confidently make updates.

To address this, I worked with the team to identify common content needs and designed a custom WordPress editing experience built around reusable content modules. Editors could create pages using pre-designed components with no need for manual styling, allowing more staff members to contribute while preserving a consistent visual presentation. We were even able to automate event publishing from a linked spreadsheet, allowing editors to keep information up to date without logging into WordPress at all.

  <div class="cs-image-block image-radius-standard image-inner-shadow image-inner-shadow-light">
    <img src="/assets/design/2020-caleitc4me-04-event-01.jpg" alt="An example of a Tax Prep Assistance event listing from the 2019 tax season refresh of CalEITC4Me.org, designed by Stephen Cummings" loading="lazy" />
  </div>

By separating design decisions from the editing interface, the new system reduced maintenance overhead and gave the organization greater flexibility to keep information current during the busy tax season.

</section>

<section class="cs-section" markdown="1">

### Designing for Clarity and Accessibility

Because tax filing information can quickly become dense and overwhelming, I focused throughout the redesign on making content easier to navigate through stronger visual hierarchy, clearer calls to action, and a friendlier visual tone.

I recommended updates to the site's color palette to improve contrast and readability, along with a simplified navigation structure to reduce cognitive load and help visitors more quickly identify the resources most relevant to their needs. While organizational constraints limited how far those changes could go, we worked together to implement meaningful improvements while remaining aligned with the established CalEITC4Me campaign identity. Elements of the navigation approach were also incorporated in later iterations of the site.

The result was a site that felt more approachable and easier to understand for Californians seeking benefits and tax filing support for themselves and their families.
